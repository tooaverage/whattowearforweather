What a boat seat redo costs in Vancouver

Nobody in the Lower Mainland publishes upholstery prices, and there's a reason: two seats that look the same from the dock can be very different jobs once the vinyl comes off. We're not going to make up numbers. Once we've quoted enough local jobs we'll publish typical ranges here. Until then, here's what actually sets the price, so you can read any quote you get, including ours.

Vinyl grade

Marine vinyl is rated for UV and mildew, and grades vary a lot in feel and lifespan. Economy vinyl looks fine in year one and chalks out early. Good marine vinyl usually outlasts the foam under it. The grade you pick is the biggest material cost on the job.

Foam

Foam that's been wet for a few seasons doesn't dry out, it composts. If a cushion feels heavy or smells like a wet dog, the foam is done and gets replaced. New foam adds material cost but it's where most of the "wow it's like a new boat" feeling comes from.

Plywood bases

Most seats are built on plywood, and plywood on a boat eventually rots. If the base flexes or the staples have nothing left to bite, we rebuild it in marine ply. This is the cost surprise on older boats, and it's also the difference between a redo that lasts and one that sags in a year.

Stitching hours

Labour is the rest of the bill. A flat bench with one seam is quick. A helm seat with pleats, piping, French seams and eight separate panels is a day of pattern work before the sewing machine even starts. More pieces, more pleats, more hours.

Is it cheaper to buy new seats?

Sometimes, for standard runabout buckets you can order off the shelf. For bench seats, engine box cushions, sun pads and anything shaped to your boat, a redo usually wins because new ones that actually fit don't exist.

How do I get a real number?

Send two photos: one wide shot, one close-up of the worst spot. Tell us if the foam feels soft or the base flexes. We'll email you an estimate within 24 hours, and we come to your marina for pickup and reinstall.
