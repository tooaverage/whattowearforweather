Boat winterization in Vancouver: what it includes

Vancouver winters are mild until they aren't. Most years there's at least one cold snap that holds below freezing for a few days, and that's all it takes: water left in an engine block or exhaust manifold expands, and cast iron loses that argument every time. Winterization is cheap insurance against the most expensive kind of engine damage there is. Here's what a proper job covers, in order.

Fuel

Stabilizer goes in the tank and gets run through the engine, so the fuel sitting in the lines and carbs or injectors over winter doesn't varnish. Ideally the tank is topped up too, which leaves less room for condensation.

Oil and filter

The oil change happens in fall, not spring. Used oil carries acids and moisture, and letting that sit in the engine for five months is how bearings corrode. Fresh oil in, run briefly to circulate, done.

Raw-water cooling

This is the part that saves the block. The raw-water side gets flushed, drained, and filled with non-toxic marine antifreeze, through the engine, the manifolds and risers, and anything else that holds seawater. On carbureted engines we fog the cylinders while we're at it.

Drives and lower units

Gear oil gets checked and changed. Milky oil means water got past a seal, and finding that in October beats finding it at the first launch of spring. Sterndrive bellows and fittings get a look, and grease points get grease.

Batteries and water systems

Batteries get topped up and either put on a maintenance charger or taken home. Fresh-water systems, heads and any onboard tanks get drained and antifreezed so a burst line doesn't ruin the cabin sole.

Staying in the water?

Normal here, and it changes the checklist a bit: bilge pumps and float switches get tested, shore power and chargers get checked, and outboards get stored tilted down so they self-drain. We do all of it at your slip.

How much does winterization cost in Vancouver?

Depends on the engine count, drive type and whether the oil change and water systems are included. Local shops sell flat-rate packages and so will we. Tell us your engine and we'll email you an estimate within 24 hours.

When should I book?

September and October. The first hard frost is the deadline nobody knows the date of, and slots go first to whoever asked earliest.
