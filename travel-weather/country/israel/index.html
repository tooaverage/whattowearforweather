Israel's weather in a nutshell

Israel is small but climatically varied, and the ratings here use Tel Aviv on the Mediterranean coast. Spring and autumn bring the gentlest weather, ideal for the beaches, the old cities and the long days of sightseeing.

Summer is hot and sticky on the coast and very hot down at the Dead Sea and Eilat, while winter is mild but the wettest time, with cool, rainy days in the north and Jerusalem. The desert south and the Red Sea stay warm and dry far later into the year.

Where you go changes when you go

Tel Aviv and the coast

Mediterranean climate, best in spring and autumn. Summer is hot and humid, winter mild and showery.

Jerusalem and the hills

Cooler and drier than the coast, lovely in spring and autumn, cold and wet in the depths of winter.

The Dead Sea, Negev and Eilat

Hot desert that stays warm and dry into winter, ideal for the Red Sea when the rest of the country cools.

Best time to visit Israel for...

Jerusalem and the old cities

March to May and September to November for warm, comfortable days exploring on foot.

Tel Aviv beaches

Late spring and early autumn for warm sea and sunshine without the peak summer humidity.

The desert and Dead Sea

The shoulder months and winter, when the south is warm and dry while the coast cools.

Eilat and the Red Sea

Warm and sunny much of the year, a winter sun and diving escape.

Israel travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Israel?
Spring, March to May, and autumn, September to November, with warm, comfortable weather for the cities, coast and desert.
Is summer a good time to visit Israel?
The coast is hot and humid and the desert is extreme, but it is beach season in Tel Aviv. Spring and autumn are easier for sightseeing.
When is the best time for the Dead Sea and Eilat?
The cooler months from autumn to spring, when the desert south is warm and dry rather than scorching.
