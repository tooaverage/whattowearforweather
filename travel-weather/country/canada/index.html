When to go to Canada

Go in summer, June to August, for the warmest weather and longest days, or in September and October for autumn colour and fewer crowds. November to March is long and cold, but it is ski and northern-lights season.

Canada's weather in a nutshell

Canada is a summer country for most visitors. The ratings here use Toronto, where June to August brings warm, lively days and September stays pleasant before the cold sets in. The far north and the prairies are harsher, the Pacific coast around Vancouver is milder and wetter.

Winter is long and serious across most of the country, from November well into March, which is exactly the draw if you have come for skiing, snow and the aurora. Spring and autumn are the value seasons, with mild weather and lighter crowds.

Where you go changes when you go

Toronto, Montreal and the east

Warm, festival-filled summers and a brilliant autumn in late September and October. Winters are cold and snowy.

The Rockies and the west

July and August for hiking around Banff and Lake Louise, December to March for world-class skiing at Whistler.

Vancouver and the coast

Mild but wet most of the year. Summer is the dry, glorious exception, which is why it is so popular.

Best time to visit Canada for...

Cities and road trips

June to early September, when the weather is warm, the patios are open and the days are long.

Autumn colour

Late September and October, especially in Quebec and Ontario, with crisp air and golden maples.

Skiing and the aurora

December to March for the Rockies and the best chance of northern lights in the north.

Value and quiet

May and September offer mild weather and better prices than the July to August peak.

Canada travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Canada?
Summer, June to August, for the warmest, liveliest weather, or September and October for autumn colour and fewer crowds. Insiders often pick the fall for its mild weather and quiet.
When is the cheapest time to visit Canada?
Late autumn and the non-holiday parts of winter, plus the spring shoulder, when crowds thin and prices drop.
Is Canada worth visiting in winter?
Yes, if you have come for it. Winters are cold but bring superb skiing in the Rockies, snowy city festivals, and a strong chance of the northern lights up north.
