Colombia's weather in a nutshell

Colombia sits on the equator, so it has no real seasons in the northern sense, just drier and wetter stretches, and the temperature is set by altitude rather than the calendar. The ratings here use Cartagena on the hot Caribbean coast, where the driest, sunniest months run from December to March.

Up in the mountains, Bogota and Medellin stay spring-like all year, cool and pleasant, while the Amazon and Pacific are hot and wet. There are roughly two drier windows, December to March and again around July and August, that travellers tend to aim for.

Where you go changes when you go

Cartagena and the Caribbean

Hot and humid, sunniest and driest from December to March. The wettest months come in the second half of the year.

Bogota and the Andes

Cool and spring-like year round thanks to the altitude, with brighter, drier spells around December to February and July.

The coffee region and Medellin

Mild and green, often called the land of eternal spring, pleasant in almost any month.

Best time to visit Colombia for...

The Caribbean coast

December to March for the sunniest, driest days in Cartagena, the beaches and Tayrona park.

Cities and culture

The drier windows of December to February and July for Bogota, Medellin and the coffee region.

Whale watching

July to October on the Pacific coast, when humpback whales come to breed.

Hiking and the coffee triangle

The drier months for clearer mountain views and firmer trails.

Colombia travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Colombia?
The drier months from December to March, with sunny weather on the Caribbean coast and in the highland cities. A second drier window falls around July and August.
Does Colombia have seasons?
Not in the usual sense. On the equator the temperature depends on altitude, and the year splits into drier and wetter spells rather than summer and winter.
When is the best time for Cartagena?
December to March, the driest and sunniest stretch on the hot Caribbean coast.
