When to go to Japan

Go in spring, late March to May, for cherry blossom and mild days, or in November for crisp air and red maple leaves. Avoid August to October, which is hot, wet and typhoon-prone.

Japan's weather in a nutshell

Japan is a long, thin country. Its islands stretch nearly three thousand kilometres from the cool, snowy reaches of Hokkaido in the north to the subtropical beaches of Okinawa in the south, so a single date can mean snowshoes in one place and swimsuits in another. What ties the country together is a strong four-season rhythm: a celebrated spring, a hot and humid summer split by a rainy season, a clear and colourful autumn, and a crisp, dry winter.

Two summer quirks shape any trip. The first is tsuyu, the plum rains, a damp grey spell that sweeps north across most of the country through June and into July. The second is typhoon season, which builds from August, peaks in September, and fades through October, mostly along the Pacific coast and the southern islands. Plan around those two and the rest of the year is remarkably kind to travellers.

Where you go changes when you go

Hokkaido, the north

Months behind the rest of the country. It largely escapes the June rains, blooms in early May rather than April, and turns into a powder-snow paradise from December to March.

Tokyo and Kansai, the heart

The classic route through Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka follows the ratings on this page closely. Spring and November are glorious and crowded, winter is clear and quiet.

Okinawa, the south

Subtropical and warm year round. Beach season runs April to October, but it sits squarely in the typhoon path from August, so aim for late spring or early summer.

Best time to visit Japan for...

Cherry blossom

Late March to mid April in Tokyo and Kyoto. Chase the bloom northward and you can still catch it in Hokkaido in early May. Book months ahead, because this is the single busiest window of the year: Tokyo hotels for late March

Autumn leaves

November across most of Honshu. Kyoto's temples and the mountains around Nikko are spectacular, and the crowds, while real, are a touch thinner than in spring.

Skiing and snow

January and February for the lightest powder in Hokkaido and the Japan Alps. Resorts often run into March, and the deep cold keeps the snow dry: Hokkaido stays in January

Beaches and the islands

Late April to early July in Okinawa, before the typhoons take hold. The water stays warm into autumn, but the storm risk climbs sharply from August.

Smaller crowds and lower prices

January, early February and the rainy stretch of June. The weather is cold or damp rather than extreme, which makes it a fair trade for quieter temples and cheaper flights.

What is on, and when

Cherry blossom, late Mar to May

The country pauses for hanami, blossom-viewing picnics under the trees. Parks, riversides and castle moats are at their best.

Autumn colour, November

Koyo, the turning of the leaves, lights up temple gardens and mountain valleys in red and gold.

Watch out

Typhoon season

August to October, worst in September. Build slack into plans on the Pacific coast and Okinawa.

Tsuyu rains

June into July. Warm and grey with downpours. Hokkaido largely escapes it.

Summer heat

July and August are hot and very humid in the cities. Head to the mountains or the far north to cool off.

Japan travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Japan?
Spring, from late March to May, for cherry blossom and mild weather, and November for clear skies and autumn colour. Both rate great to ideal on weather, though they are also the busiest and priciest times.
What is the cheapest time to visit Japan?
January, early February and the rainy stretch of June offer the lowest prices and thinnest crowds. The weather is cold or damp rather than extreme, so it is a fair trade for the savings.
Is it worth visiting Japan in winter?
Yes. Winters are cold but mostly dry and clear, with world-class skiing in Hokkaido and the Japan Alps, hot springs to warm up in, and far fewer tourists than spring.
Should I avoid Japan during typhoon season?
You do not have to, but plan for disruption. Typhoons run August to October and peak in September, mainly hitting Okinawa and the Pacific coast. Travel insurance and a flexible itinerary help a lot.
