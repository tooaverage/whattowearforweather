When to go to Croatia

Go in May to June or September to October for warm seas, sunshine and far fewer crowds than the July to August peak. Winter is mild on the coast but quiet, with many islands winding down.

Croatia's weather in a nutshell

Croatia is a Mediterranean country built for the shoulder seasons. The ratings here use Split on the Dalmatian coast, where late spring and early autumn bring warm, swimmable seas and sunshine without the August crush.

High summer is hot, gorgeous and very busy along the coast and islands, with peak prices to match. Winter is mild but sleepy, especially on the islands, while the inland capital of Zagreb and the Plitvice lakes stay rewarding into the colder months.

Where you go changes when you go

Split, Dubrovnik and the coast

The heart of a Croatian trip, best from May to June and September to October. July and August are hot and packed.

The Dalmatian islands

Hvar, Brac and the rest are liveliest in summer. Late spring and early autumn offer warm seas with breathing room.

Zagreb and Plitvice

The inland capital and the famous lakes are lovely in spring and autumn, with the lakes spectacular after the snowmelt.

Best time to visit Croatia for...

Beaches and islands

June and September for warm seas and sunshine on the Dalmatian coast, on either side of the August peak.

Dubrovnik and old towns

May to June and September to October for comfortable sightseeing without the cruise-day crush.

Sailing

Late spring to early autumn, when the seas are warm and the harbours less frantic than midsummer.

Plitvice Lakes

Spring for full waterfalls after the snowmelt, or autumn for golden colour and fewer visitors.

What is on, and when

Croatia travel FAQ

What is the best time to visit Croatia?
Late spring and early autumn, May to June and September to October, with warm seas, sunshine and smaller crowds than July and August.
Is the sea warm enough to swim?
The Adriatic is comfortable for swimming from roughly June into October, warmest in late summer after a season of sun.
Can you visit the Croatian islands in winter?
You can, but many hotels, ferries and restaurants on the smaller islands wind down. The coastal cities stay open and quiet.
